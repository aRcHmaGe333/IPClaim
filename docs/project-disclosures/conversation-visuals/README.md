# Conversation Visuals

## Core idea

A system listens to speech during a lecture, presentation, talk, panel, public discussion, demonstration, class, broadcast, exhibition, or other spoken event and continuously supplies relevant visuals based on what is being said.

The visuals can appear on stage screens, projection surfaces, audience displays, streamed overlays, or other presentation surfaces.

The primary purpose is to make spoken communication richer and easier to follow without forcing the speaker to prepare every visual in advance.

## Example behaviour

A lecturer moves from architecture to a particular city, then to an artist, an unusual machine, a historical event, and an imagined future object.

The system can respond by surfacing or generating relevant visuals for each transition: photographs, diagrams, maps, artwork, animation, generated imagery, video fragments, data visualisations, archival material, or other useful visual forms.

A presenter can mention an unfamiliar person, place, object, process, artwork, scientific idea, historical event, or comparison and the audience can immediately see supporting visual material while the speaker continues.

The result is not simply subtitles beside a speaker. The visual layer becomes a live extension of the spoken material.

## Basic flow

1. Capture the live speech.
2. Convert it into usable language and context.
3. Track the current subject, entities, references, claims, examples, and topic changes.
4. Decide which visual form would add the most value at that moment.
5. Retrieve, generate, or compose suitable visual material.
6. Present it with timing that supports rather than interrupts the speaker.
7. Continue updating as the talk develops.

## Primary environments

The strongest use cases include:

- lectures and classrooms;
- conference presentations;
- talks and panels;
- public discussions and debates;
- demonstrations and workshops;
- museums and exhibitions;
- cultural events;
- streamed talks and broadcasts;
- public meetings;
- live educational content.

## Broader use

The same mechanism can also work in more informal or private settings where live conversation would benefit from a responsive visual layer, including studios, collaborative workspaces, meetings, homes, and social or creative venues.

## Relationship to Source-to-Visual Video

Conversation Visuals is live and reactive.

[Source-to-Visual Video](../source-to-visual-video/README.md) applies a related idea to a bounded source such as a chat log, document, project specification, README, transcript, or article and turns that source into a designed visual sequence that can become a video.

## Deployment considerations

Real-world implementations should make microphone use, consent, retention, and privacy controls visible and understandable to the people in the space.

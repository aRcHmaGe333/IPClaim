# Accelerated

## Core idea

People and organisations with extraordinary compute, model access, engineering capacity, research infrastructure, and execution resources can openly accept promising prospective projects from the public and **accelerate the projects themselves**.

The project is the unit being accelerated.

This goes beyond a conventional startup accelerator, incubator, mentorship programme, or grant. A submitted project can be actively worked forward: researched, analysed, simulated, designed, prototyped, tested, debugged, documented, and prepared for real deployment at a pace that may be impossible for an individual originator or small team.

## Basic flow

1. A person submits a project, invention, research direction, technical problem, cultural project, public-interest system, or other serious prospective work.
2. The receiving organisation evaluates whether the project is worth accelerating.
3. Accepted work receives concentrated compute and execution capacity.
4. Agents, models, researchers, engineers, tools, simulation, prototyping, and other available resources attack the project's actual bottlenecks.
5. The project returns to its originator materially advanced: with solved questions, working artifacts, evidence, implementation, or a clear next state.

## Why this exists

Extreme compute concentration creates a large difference between what can be conceived and what an individual can practically resolve.

A person may have a valuable project while lacking years of engineering time, access to specialist knowledge, simulation capacity, hardware, capital, or enough parallel attention. Meanwhile some organisations possess enough compute and technical capacity to compress large amounts of that work into a very short period.

The proposal is to make part of that capacity openly available to prospective projects.

## Key distinction

The promise is not merely:

> We will help you become better at working on your project.

It is:

> Submit the project. We will accelerate the project.

## Open design questions

The project intentionally leaves several choices open for later design, including:

- selection and ranking of submitted projects;
- compute allocation;
- human and model oversight;
- ownership and licensing arrangements;
- confidentiality and public-disclosure options;
- safety and feasibility review;
- treatment of public-interest versus commercial projects;
- funding and revenue models;
- how completed work is returned, continued, licensed, funded, or deployed.

Those choices affect implementation. They are not the core invention.
